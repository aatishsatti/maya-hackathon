The scam check: the reasoning setting, and what changing it cost

The check on typed and spoken talk was changed from reasoning low to minimal. The decision was made on the dev half only: 76 items, 4 runs per setting.

The cost of the change, stated plainly. On the held-out half, which was run once for each setting, minimal warned 37 of 38 scams against 38 of 38 for low (35 of 38 against 36 of 38 behind the cheap word filter that runs live). The one difference is a polite invoice scam ("our bank details have changed") that moved from a warning to a caution: still flagged, not missed. In exchange the check is about 1.7 times faster and uses 57% fewer output tokens. The intervals overlap, so this is not a significant drop, but it is not nothing either. Hearing: Meta Transcribe

Twenty-four fixed sentences were each spoken by two of four built-in Mac voices (US, UK, Australian, Irish) and transcribed by Meta Transcribe: 48 hearings, 0 failed. Word error rate 3.0% (the average of 8 runs, 384 hearings, because Transcribe varies a little from run to run); 98.5% of the key words survived, and 99.4% of the words a scam turns on (gift cards, Medicare number, date of birth, code, arrested, install). Average 2.1 s per voice note.

Limit. This is synthetic clean speech made by the Mac's built-in voice: no real older voices, no accents, no hesitation, no noisy room. It shows the ears work and the key words come through; it does not show real-world accuracy. Most errors are a repeated word ("are finished are finished"), and once the name Maya came back as Ma. The live streamed-call path of the same model was not measured separately.

Scorecard

Each rule is a guard rail the evaluation checks on every run, so a later change cannot quietly make Maya worse.

#1 Conversation

  • pass no connect / close reply asks a question, and a close is one sentence (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass Spark sends topic= on at least 97% of replies (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass topic= is right on at least 90% of clear turns (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass personal= is right on at least 85% of labelled turns (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass repeat rate at most 10% (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass the "nobody knows" worry is answered with what the app does at least 80% of the time (3 of 3 runs)

#3 Memory

  • pass a due follow-up is asked at the first reply in at least half of the calls (it was 13 of 16 over four full runs) (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass a follow-up is never asked twice in one call (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass the code never offers a follow-up in a heavy moment or a safety moment (a guarantee, always 100%) (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass trimmed memory still answers son / tea / where he lives (at least 90%) (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass nothing is invented about a daughter or a job that are not in memory (at least 75%: one unlucky reply of eight is noise) (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass when she asked, an answered follow-up is closed, a brushed-off one is closed at least half of the time, and a closed one is not asked again (3 of 3 runs)

#2 Trends

  • pass ordinary life is wrongly flagged at most 4% of the time (3% for pure noise) (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass slow drifts are noticed within two weeks at least 72% of the time on average (3 of 3 runs)

#4 Scam shield

  • pass dev scams warned at least 94% (36 of 38) (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass no normal item is warned or paused (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass scams in progress are paused at least 70% of the time (7 of 10); genuine payments and other normal items never are (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass the word filter checks every dev scam and fires on at most 2 of 30 everyday lines (3 of 3 runs)

Hearing (Meta Transcribe)

  • pass Meta Transcribe: word error rate on clean synthetic speech at most 6% (it has been 2.5% to 3.3%) (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass Meta Transcribe: at least 95% of the key words survive, and at least 95% of the scam words (3 of 3 runs)
  • pass Meta Transcribe: no sentence fails to come back (3 of 3 runs)

Meta calls

  • pass real errors from Meta or the network are at most 2% of calls (cancelled replies are not errors) (3 of 3 runs)

Honest limits

  • The test sets were written by the team that built the features, and the same family of model reads them. Real conversations, real scammers and real older voices may be harder.
  • Small samples: 38 scams and 38 normal items per half; "0 wrongly warned" can hide a true rate of up to about 9%. Run-to-run noise is about two items.
  • Simulations (trends) show the rules behave sensibly under a stated model, not that they work on real people.
  • Photos (Spark vision) are wired and used, but are not measured here. The streamed call path of Transcribe is likewise not measured.
  • About 26% of live replies are cancelled and rewritten by Maya's own repeat and beat guards: that is why the repeat rate is near zero, and it costs extra time and tokens.

Run it yourself

npm test runs the offline checks (no network). npm run eval runs the Meta evaluation (text only, no ElevenLabs, about 5 minutes) and exits with an error if a guard rail fails or a number has dropped. node eval/page.js rebuilds this page.
